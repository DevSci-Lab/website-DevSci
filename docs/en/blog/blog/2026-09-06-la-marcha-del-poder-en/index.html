The March of Power | Resistance, Nature and Diversity

By Silvia Ramírez | 2026-09-06 | Filosofía, Sociedad

They wanted to make us feel weak, but they only filled me with strength. It is a strength we created, a "supernatural" resistance... and conveniently, they call it resistance to simply going along with the natural order, when this is in fact the truth of being.

It was as if, suddenly, I were the witch extracting all that remained of the human in them, and that is when I understood: historically, witches were not burned for their spells — they were burned for the terror that power in their eyes, in their actions, and in their deep connection to their surroundings provoked. Those who impose order fear the power of the multitude because they know that, at the end of the day, they are fewer.

From so much helplessness and anxiety in the face of the reality in which we survive — a world where it sometimes seems impossible to desire being — I decided to seek refuge in literature. I set out to reread the supernatural stories of Carlos Fuentes. What better narrative to describe our present than a terrifying story, full of signs of how badly everything has gone wrong? Fuentes used horror to strip reality bare, and in that same reflection I understood George Orwell's true warning: the absolute danger is the attempt to manipulate everything from a single community, imposing a single way of existing.

Today, that Orwellian dystopia takes shape in our natural spaces. This is a social order built on monoculture and isolation. The drive to impose a hegemonic model of development is the ecological equivalent of monoculture: razing the richness of an environment to plant a single idea, drying out the soil and making it fragile. Social networks and collective narrative dictate what you must think without granting you any decision of your own — it is like exhausting the land without rest, isolating it, and so there is no renewal of thought; only the single logical drop of the environment we mistake for reality.

In reality, survival is collaborative. From the invisible bacterial consortia that nourish the subsoil to the groups of farmers, mariners, and scientists — life is a network where everyone contributes. But it seems that today we live a reality dictated by social control rather than by human and natural collaboration. That is why truly caring for diversity demands rejecting the impositions of a mold. Natural spaces and life itself must be protected by adapting to the nature of what they are and to their intrinsic strengths, without imitating the struggles or conditions of other ecosystems. Conservation ideas cannot be "tropicalized"; the environment must be allowed to evolve free from extractivist ideologies — and so, diversity is salvation.
